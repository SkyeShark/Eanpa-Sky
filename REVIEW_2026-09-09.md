## Reflections and puddle motion

The reflection tracer now intersects **current-frame geometry**. Only the radiance is taken from history, reprojected with camera/object motion and validated against object identity and previous depth. Invalid history uses the existing local probe/sky fallback inside Three's native PBR lighting. Chrome, material maps, Fresnel, clearcoat and the other native BRDF lobes remain in place.

A Three skinning-history bug was fixed as well: if a shadow or probe variant updates a skeleton before the main material, it now preserves the previous bones needed by the reflection motion pass. Capture variants follow source material changes and release resources when their source is disposed.

- [Rigid-motion GPU contract](qa/review/feedback-20260909/reflection-motion-contract.json): independent CPU transforms agree with GPU motion within 0.002 screen pixels; sampled main/reflection depth matches exactly.
- [Close-orb trace](qa/review/feedback-20260909/orb-trace-contract.json): 29,283 accepted surrounding-geometry hits, zero hits on the orb's own convex receiver group.
- [Chrome-arm motion check](qa/review/feedback-20260909/chrome-motion-contract.json): in twelve small camera moves, 99% of sampled arm pixels match the settled frame exactly; average normalized radiance differences are below 0.000008.
- [Chrome/orb motion](qa/review/feedback-20260909/chrome-orb-walk.webm) and [walking puddles](qa/review/feedback-20260909/puddles-walking.webm), both at normal simulation speed.

The [final full-scene repeat](qa/review/feedback-20260909/chrome-motion-final.json)
also has zero difference at the 99th percentile of sampled arm pixels, with
average normalized differences below 0.00011 in its different pose. The
[SSR positive control](qa/review/feedback-20260909/ssr-positive-control.json)
changes 1,811 image pixels by more than two 8-bit channel values when only SSR
is disabled, including 1,069 in the arms region. Restoring SSR reproduces the
original image exactly. This confirms an active local-reflection contribution.

Completed local reflection probes crossfade for 0.45 seconds, including their distinct projection boxes. Rain exposure retains a fixed world grid within a guard area instead of shifting puddle and shelter boundaries at the capture's slower refresh cadence. Moving roofs and other occluders still refresh at the configured rate.

These checks are bounded regression cases. Screen space still cannot reconstruct hidden/off-screen geometry, and newly revealed samples may briefly use the fallback. This is not multi-bounce ray tracing.

## Cloud motion, shadows and Ringworld

Cloud position is integrated from wind velocity. Changing weather therefore changes velocity without multiplying the entire elapsed session time by a new speed. Distant ring clouds use the same displacement, with circumference and ribbon width converted independently to metres. Their cached texture advects between captures.

The high cloud sheets use a continuous atmospheric shell, removing the finite local deck's horizon cut. Eclipse visibility is evaluated per visible ring fragment, so the near ring receives the midday shadow. Sunlit far-arc clouds keep their daylight illumination during local night.

- [Near ring at noon](qa/review/feedback-20260909/ring-noon-final.png).
- [Cirrus horizon](qa/review/feedback-20260909/ring-cirrus-final.png).
- [Night with cumulus](qa/review/feedback-20260909/ring-night-cumulus-final.png).
- [Distant-cloud movement](qa/review/feedback-20260909/ring-distant-clouds.webm): 8 seconds recorded at **8x simulation speed**, explicitly accelerated to expose distant advection.

Cloud shadows are a shared light-column field sampled by arbitrary receiving geometry. The light-plane height follows the projected host volume, preserving useful ground/roof resolution at sunset. Each capture's basis stays perpendicular to its sun direction throughout gradual day cycles. A guard area keeps the grid stable during ordinary walking at a fixed sun angle.

Two completed captures share one atlas and one texture/sampler binding. The receiver evaluates each field in its own world projection, then blends them. This costs two texture reads during blending, one when settled, and avoids a cloud ray march in every receiving fragment. Active weather spends most frames in the blend. It also avoids filtering the previous image repeatedly, which produced a small residual edge shift in an earlier version of this pass.

[The same-time publication test](qa/review/feedback-20260909/shield-shadow-motion-contract.json) compares the shadow immediately before and after each refresh at 4,096 world receivers over 2.4 km, including ground and 25 m roofs. Across twenty refreshes it measured **zero publication change**, with hundreds of partially shadowed receivers in every sample. [The controlled GPU blend test](qa/review/feedback-20260909/shadow-publication-contract.json) confirms the old value is retained at publication, interpolates halfway, and reaches the new value.

Performance uses a 256 x 256 integration field per capture; Balanced and High use 384 x 384. That reduces Performance's capture texels by 56%. The atlas stores two captures; it does not double the cloud integration work per refresh. The map remains finite and filtered: it is not per-pixel cloud ray tracing, and tall receivers near sharp cloud edges can differ from direct integration.

## Rain, surface response and weather changes

[The rain-surface GPU contract](qa/review/feedback-20260909/rain-surface-contract.json) passes roofs, slopes, instancing, deformation, skinning, cutouts, slanted rain, shelter, source disposal and failed-capture recovery. Moving two metres retains the projection; crossing the guard recentres it.

[The sunshower cell contract](qa/review/feedback-20260909/rain-cell-contract.json) checks ripple normals in raining and dry regions at two times. Raining cells animate impacts; the dry cells contain zero impact slope. Wetness can remain after rain stops, while new droplet impacts require current rain supply.

All three sky factories accept `weatherOptions` for host collision roots and impact callbacks. [The generic host strike](qa/review/feedback-20260909/lightning-host.json) reached an upward mesh surface and delivered its impact record, without a demonstration terrain object. ## Performance scope and method

No sample mesh batching or asset simplification is counted in this pass. `qa/sky-fixture.html` uses six generic host meshes and the application's actual sky factories, weather, reflection pipeline and quality definitions. It includes precipitation, surface capture, cloud shadows, volumetric clouds, native reflections, shared post-processing and periodic sky/PMREM rebakes.

Shared post-processing savings are reported separately from cloud rendering. Non-accumulating AO no longer clears/copies an unused temporal history. Bloom skips its blur pyramid when the visible emissive attachment is provably empty; dynamic emissive nodes and custom MRT outputs remain eligible. These savings depend on host content and enabled effects. The generic fixture has no emissive surfaces, so bloom is skipped there; the demonstration orb remains eligible.

Measurements use the RTX 5090 Laptop GPU (24 GB, 150 W limit, driver 610.88) at a 1578 x 846 drawing buffer, with camera movement. Each run includes a scheduled sky/PMREM refresh halfway through its 20-second interval, plus the normal tier cadence. The runner reuses one browser and runs scenarios serially.

| Resource profile | Browser CPU | Competing GPU workload |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Native | Normal | None injected |
| Moderate | 2x CDP slowdown | Calibrated toward 4 ms per frame |
| Limited | 4x CDP slowdown | Calibrated toward 8 ms per frame |

The synthetic workload permits at most two submissions in flight and records its actual GPU duration. It reserves processing time on the same GPU; it does not emulate another architecture, driver, memory capacity or thermal limit, and is not an FPS prediction for a named card. The frame-function timer includes submission and intentional budget waits, so it is not isolated CPU execution time. Separate GPU timestamps sum actual render/compute passes and exclude queue idle time.

Unrelated CPU/GPU activity is recorded. The final comparison gate requires external CPU below 5% of total logical-processor capacity and external GPU engines below 5%. Earlier exploratory runs used a looser CPU gate; those are not relabelled as quiet baselines. Contended rates must not be compared against quiet runs to claim a tier regression or speedup.
