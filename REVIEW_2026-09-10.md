# Red giant correction, CPU investigation and rain arrival

Follow-up to the September 9 review, included in v0.2.0.

## Rain transition timing

The None-to-Rain fade raised global wetness and pooled-water targets before the
cloud above the listener produced rain. A real-time reproduction had local rain
cell zero at 30 seconds while measured surface wetness was already 0.439.
Drops and audio correctly waited for local cloud coverage; the surface response
was following a different supply.

Surface wetness and water now accumulate in a world-aligned GPU history from
the same local rain-cell function. Thin wetness develops first, pooled water
follows, and both remain at their world location when a rain cloud moves away.
The existing arbitrary-mesh exposure, slope, cavity and PBR response still apply.
Rain-free overcast humidity can damp a surface without filling puddles.

[Transition captures and raw measurements](qa/review/rain-timing-20260910/index.html)
show the corrected sequence in the actual Balanced Earth sky with generic host
geometry: dry at 20 seconds; local rain and slight wetness at 30 seconds with no
puddle response; accumulated water after sustained rainfall. These runs use
normal simulation speed but different cloud phases, so before/after arrival
times are not a controlled speed comparison. The baseline's puddle diagnostic
was invalid and is excluded; its wetness and local-cell channels were valid.

- The numerical GPU contract passes 23 checks covering wet/dry columns, upper
  surfaces, filling, drying, camera recentering, continuous response between
  updates, duplicate-time refresh and clock rewind.
- All 70 Node tests pass, including three new accumulation lifecycle tests.
- The complete Balanced Earth standalone also renders rain and puddles with
  its authored mapped materials and no shader errors. The audio context runs
  with all 38 assets loaded and local rain supply 0.7. Inspection uses the one
  owned headless browser; pointer lock remains off.
- History costs 1 MiB on Balanced/High and 0.25 MiB on Performance. Four updates
  per simulation second are shared by all receivers; receiver moisture evolves
  every displayed frame. No CPU readback or per-object accumulation update.
- A native 5090 timestamp capture measured eight history passes: mean 0.0090 ms,
  maximum 0.0226 ms. This measures the history pass only, excluding its extra
  texture reads in receiver shaders. It is not an end-to-end speedup or a
  simulated lower-end device benchmark.

The finite moisture footprint starts newly entered areas dry. Rainfall history
uses horizontal columns at reference height zero, so very tall structures in
strong wind can differ from exact per-height rain coverage. Exposure is still
surface-specific. This is not runoff or persistent per-object water simulation;
the host controls and limits are documented in [engine/RAIN.md](engine/RAIN.md).
Audio arrival was checked through the shared listener supply, not by claiming
to hear the headless browser.

## Red giant

The thin magnetic arches have been replaced with the earlier radiant prominence
shader from `2cfca90`: nested fibrous bands, bright cores, broad glow, moving
filaments and eruptive streamers. Front-facing events retain their existing
surface anchors and now use that same shading, clipped against a local surface
plane. The original five limb-event schedules are restored. The three front
events retain their schedules and paired sunspots.

The surface retains `granScale = 52`, radial foreshortening, angular radius,
colour ramp and pink limb. The plasma clock was advancing, but the combination
of a 0.15 speed factor and a reduction of deformation from 1.9 to 0.85 made its
motion too subdued. Deformation is restored to 1.9 cell units and animation now
runs at half the earlier visible rate. The finer spatial scale makes that
motion smaller in angular size without also suppressing the deformation.

[Before/after videos and full-sky captures](qa/review/redgiant-20260910/index.html)
use normal simulation speed. One owned headless browser was reused serially.
The isolated views use the production celestial shader at 960 x 720, 52-degree
FOV, ACES and exposure 1. The integrated views use the actual Balanced
Shieldworld factory and shared rendering pipeline, with generic host geometry,
under clear and cumulus skies. The shield remains visible in both.

- Four matching-time comparisons contain 254,772 pixels each outside the limb.
  Every channel matches the earlier radiant-flare reference exactly.
- Across 46,424 interior pixels, a half-second advance changes mean displayed
  channel values by 10.66/255, compared with 2.31/255 before. This checks visible
  motion; it is not an optical-flow speed measurement or a realism score.
- The full sky's star clock matches its simulation time and reports no render
  errors. Captures and their metadata retain the normal playback rate.
- All 67 existing Node tests pass, with syntax and whitespace checks passing.

## CPU findings

The quality label is a workload target, not a frame-rate cap. The main loop in
`src/main.js` attempts a frame on every available animation callback when the
previous render task has completed. Producing 100–120 frames per second means
preparing roughly twice as many frames as producing 60, even on Balanced.

The saved `fixture-sky-4x.cpuprofile` is a previous CPU-throttled sky/effects
fixture capture, not a new native-device measurement. Its sampled stacks point
primarily to renderer object setup, uniform/buffer updates, render-pass setup
and GPU command submission. This supports investigating CPU overhead in the
shared rendering pipeline; it does not mean the cloud ray marcher executes on
the CPU. A main-thread profile also does not cover all CPU activity in Chrome's
separate GPU process.

A fresh five-second process sample, taken before starting this inspection
browser, measured Chrome at about 16.4% of total logical-processor capacity.
Two renderer processes and Chrome's GPU process accounted for most of that
sample. The process list does not identify which tab owns each renderer, so
that total cannot be attributed to Eanpa alone. Eanpa's Python server was nearly
idle. The busy Python process was a Blender multiprocessing child. No unrelated
process was stopped.

Startup and quality/world replacement also precompile material variants.
[Three's renderer documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Renderer.html#compileAsync)
describes this precompilation as a way to move first-use shader stalls out of
rendering. These startup costs must be profiled separately from steady play.

This follow-up does not claim a CPU speedup. CPU optimization should measure
the reusable sky/weather pipeline's submission and update costs, with fixed
frame-rate power/CPU comparisons reported separately from uncapped throughput.

## Arm zoom explanation

The wheel handler changes the world camera's FOV between 28 and 80 degrees.
The arm rig remains parented to that same camera at a fixed authored scale.
Zooming out widens the perspective rather than pulling the camera backward
along the arms. Their close-up perspective therefore changes without the
expected camera movement. No arm or camera behavior was changed in this pass.
